## Design Context

### Users
Tap is used by developers, maintainers, and agent operators working inside Copilot CLI while background commands, prompts, providers, and session events continue running. They need to diagnose "what is tap doing right now?" without interrupting their coding flow or reading scattered extension logs.

### Brand Personality
Attentive, exacting, and operational. The interface should feel like a flight recorder for agentic background work: calm under pressure, information-dense, and trustworthy rather than decorative.

### Aesthetic Direction
Industrial diagnostic console with a warm technical palette anchored by the ※ reference-mark identity. Prefer a light-first, high-contrast interface with ink, parchment, amber, and restrained red/green status accents. Avoid generic dark neon dashboards, glassmorphism, vague gradients, rounded-card sameness, and decorative charts that do not explain state.

### Design Principles
1. Show the live system shape first: streams, emitters, providers, queues, and session events should be scannable at a glance.
2. Preserve evidence: logs and events should read like a bounded flight recorder with timestamps, sources, and severity.
3. Make noise manageable: filtering, search, and compact summaries are part of the design, not afterthoughts.
4. Keep diagnostics safe: redact secrets, bound history, and avoid exposing auth tokens or full unbounded transcript payloads.
5. Design for operators in motion: keyboard focus, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support, and fast refresh behavior matter more than decoration.
